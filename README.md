# Retail Sales Analysis with Google BigQuery

# Introduction
- This project demonstrates how to use Google BigQuery to analyze a retail sales dataset. The dataset contains transactional data including customer demographics, product categories, pricing, and purchase amounts. The goal is to apply SQL queries to extract insights and perform data-driven analysis.

# Problem Statement
Retail businesses generate large volumes of transactional data. However, without proper analysis, this data remains underutilized. The challenge is to:
- Filter and aggregate data efficiently.
- Identify patterns in customer behavior.
- Classify and segment customers based on spending and demographics.
- Generate actionable insights for business decisions.

# Aim of the Project
To leverage Google BigQuery for querying and analyzing retail sales data, enabling businesses to:
 - Understand revenue trends.
 - Segment customers by age and spending.
 - Identify high-value transactions and profitable product categories.

 # Objectives
Apply SQL queries to filter, aggregate, and classify data.
Calculate key metrics such as total revenue and average spending.
Segment customers into meaningful groups (e.g., age groups, spending levels).
Identify top-performing product categories.
Generate insights for marketing and inventory strategies.

# Tools & Technologies
Google BigQuery: For cloud-based data analysis.
SQL: For querying and transforming data.
Retail Sales Dataset: CSV file uploaded to BigQuery.
GitHub: For version control and project documentation.

# Summary of Results
Successfully filtered transactions for specific conditions (e.g., year, high-value purchases).
Computed total revenue and average transaction amounts.
Classified customers into Youth, Adult, and Senior groups.
Identified product categories contributing most to revenue.
Created custom fields for Spending Level and Unit Cost Category.

# Key Insights
Electronics and Beauty categories dominate high-value transactions.
Customers aged 40+ contribute significantly to overall revenue.
High-value transactions (> $1000) are concentrated among fewer customers, indicating potential for loyalty programs.
Price segmentation reveals that most products fall into the Moderate range, but Expensive items drive revenue spikes.
